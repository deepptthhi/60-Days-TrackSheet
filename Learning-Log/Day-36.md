# Day 36 - Learning Introduction to Computer Networks

## What did I learn?

Today, I started learning **Computer Networks**, one of the core subjects in computer science and an essential topic for Backend Development, DevOps, Cloud Computing, and Site Reliability Engineering (SRE). Until now, I had been building backend applications, but I did not fully understand how computers actually communicate with each other over the internet. Today's learning helped me understand the basics of network communication.

I learned that a **computer network** is a group of interconnected devices that communicate and share resources such as files, applications, databases, and internet connectivity. I also understood why networks are important and how almost every modern application depends on them.

Another important concept I learned was the different **types of networks**—PAN, LAN, MAN, and WAN—and how they are classified based on geographical coverage. I also explored common **network topologies** such as Bus, Star, Ring, Mesh, Tree, and Hybrid, and understood why the Star topology is widely used today.

I also learned about common **network devices** like hubs, switches, routers, bridges, repeaters, gateways, modems, and access points. Understanding their individual responsibilities helped me visualize how data moves across different networks.

Towards the end of today's learning, I explored **Client-Server** and **Peer-to-Peer architectures**, along with **Packet Switching** and **Circuit Switching**. Finally, I understood the difference between **Bandwidth**, **Throughput**, and **Latency**, which are commonly used to measure network performance.

By the end of today's learning, I realized that computer networks are the backbone of modern applications, cloud platforms, and distributed systems.

## What challenges did I face?

The biggest challenge today was differentiating between **Bandwidth**, **Throughput**, and **Latency**, as they all initially seemed to represent internet speed. After studying examples, I understood their individual meanings.

I also found it slightly difficult to remember the functions of different **network devices** and distinguish between various **network topologies**, but practicing real-world examples made these concepts much clearer.
